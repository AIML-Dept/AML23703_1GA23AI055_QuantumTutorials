========================================================================
 TUTORIAL 10 | EXERCISE 4 (REAL-WORLD): 100-Message Bandwidth Study
========================================================================

Session: 100 random 2-bit messages = 200 classical bits of information
Message mix : '00' x 27, '01' x 26, '10' x 21, '11' x 26
First 10    : 11 11 01 00 11 11 10 00 11 10

[1] Simulated transmission of the full session
    Ideal channel            : 100/100 messages decoded correctly
    Bit-flip p = 0.05 (session 1): 92/100 messages decoded correctly, 8 bit errors out of 200
    First 10 decoded (ideal): 11 11 01 00 11 11 10 00 11 10
    First 10 decoded (noisy): 11 11 01 00 11 11 10 00 11 10

    Noisy session repeated 200 times (p = 0.05):
    Wrong messages per session : mean 5.09, min 0, max 12  (expected 5)
    Sessions with zero errors  : 4 / 200

[2] Bandwidth comparison for the session
Quantity                                       Classical   Superdense
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Information delivered (bits)                         200          200
Carriers sent during the session                200 bits   100 qubits
Bits delivered per carrier sent                        1            2
Pre-shared resource needed beforehand               none    100 ebits
Total carriers incl. pre-sharing                     200          200
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Channel uses during the session: 200 -> 100 (50% fewer)
Total including ebit distribution: 200 -> 200 (no net saving)

Summary:
  * During the session superdense coding halves the traffic: 100 qubits
    carry the 200 bits, because each qubit delivers 2 bits.
  * The cost is paid in advance: 100 entangled pairs must be shared
    before the session, so the total number of carriers is still 200.
  * The gain is useful when the session channel is the bottleneck and
    ebits can be distributed earlier, e.g. during off-peak time.
  * Real channels add noise: at p = 0.05, about 5.1 of 100 messages
    arrive wrong per session on average, so error correction would be needed.
